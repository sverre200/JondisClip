# File size: what can shrink without losing quality (2026-11-12)

**Question (owner):** clips are big. Can the total size go down while keeping the quality?

## What's on disk
The owner's library (`D:\JondisClip-Clips`, 23 files, 5.4 GB), all H.264 1080p60, Balanced:

| What | Files | Size |
|---|---|---|
| 5-minute originals | 8 | 4.7 GB |
| of which already have a trimmed edit | 6 | 3.0 GB |
| Trimmed edits (3–79 s) | 15 | 0.7 GB |

- The WoW originals run 11–23 Mb/s (430–900 MB per 5 minutes). The Balanced target is 21 Mb/s.
- **55 % of the library is 5-minute originals whose useful part was already cut out.**

## Encoder test
- **Encoder:** `h264_amf` / `hevc_amf`, RX 6800 XT, driver 32.0.21045.16010, the app's options (`usage=transcoding`, `quality=balanced`, `rc=vbr_peak`, max 1.5×, buffer 1×, GOP 60, `qmin 18`).
- **Footage:** two 20 s cuts from the owner's real WoW clips, stream-copied as the reference:
  - **busy:** from a 23.9 Mb/s clip (combat, camera turning).
  - **calm:** from an 11.1 Mb/s clip.
- **Metric:** VMAF v0.6.1, mean / p5 (95 % of frames score higher), each encode against its reference.
- **Caveat:** the references are themselves H.264 at the current settings, not lossless. So scores show the *extra* loss of a second encode, and H.264 re-encoding H.264 is probably flattered a little against HEVC. The differences between settings are what to read, not the absolute numbers. A lossless real-game capture would settle it.

## Findings
1. **There's no free efficiency left in the H.264 encoder on this card.**
   - High profile changes nothing (the stream is the same apart from the profile flag).
   - B-frames make it *worse*: 21.1 Mb/s with B-frames scores like 18.8 without.
   - `quality=quality` and pre-analysis are within 0.3 VMAF.
2. **In busy scenes size and quality trade directly:** each 20 % off the bitrate costs about 2 VMAF mean and 4 at p5. About 6 is where a difference becomes noticeable, so one step down (target 17) is safe; two (target 14) is borderline in the worst frames.
3. **Calm scenes have slack.** A higher quality floor (`qmin 20`) and a 2 s GOP cut 18–26 % for under 1 VMAF, and busy scenes aren't touched.
4. **HEVC is a smaller win here than its reputation:** about 10 % in busy scenes and 20 % in calm ones for the same score (with the caveat above). That doesn't pay for the playback problems of ADR-0007.
5. **What's kept matters far more than how it's encoded.** Encoder tuning can take 10–25 % off. The originals that already have an edit are 55 % of the library, and a 5-minute save for a 20-second moment is 15× more than needed.

## Options that follow
| Option | Saves | Costs |
|---|---|---|
| Balanced: `qmin` 18 → 20 | ~18 % in calm scenes, 0 in busy | under 1 VMAF in calm scenes |
| GOP 1 s → 2 s | ~10 % in calm scenes | saves start up to 2 s earlier than asked; touches the segment writer |
| Balanced target 0.17 → 0.14 bits/pixel | ~20 % everywhere | ~2 VMAF in busy scenes |
| Offer to recycle the original after a trim; a "clean up" list | up to 55 % of this library | none (explicit, to the Recycle Bin) |
| A second hotkey that saves a shorter length | most of a save, when used | a second global hotkey |

## Decision (owner, 2026-11-12)
The owner chose the two encoder options (ADR-0024):
- **Quality floor up by 2:** Performance 22, Balanced 20, High 18 (AMD only, as before).
- **Balanced H.264 target 0.17 → 0.136 bits/pixel** (1080p60: 21.2 → 16.9 Mb/s). HEVC stays at 0.12, which scored the same as H.264 at the new target (92.5 against 92.6).

Not chosen: recycling originals after a trim, and a short-save hotkey. GOP stays 1 s.

**Check after the change:** `--example record` for 20 s on the test profile opened `h264_amf` 1920×1080@60 at 16.9 Mb/s, encoded 1201 frames with 0 late and 0 dropped, CPU 0.67 % of the machine, working set 80 MB.

**Not measured:** the same settings on Intel (QSV) and NVIDIA. The lower target applies there too; the quality floor doesn't.
